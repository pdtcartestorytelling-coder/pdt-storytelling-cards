PDT IN PRACTICE

Three settings. Three ways to tell a story.

How might a photograph fit into a professional conversation? Explore three fictional situations with open questions and considerations for facilitators.

Examples created for this presentation: the situations, described images and questions are fictional, not real cases, reproductions of the original sheets or validated clinical instructions. The described images are not necessarily part of the PdT deck.

01 · Individual

Individual work · A time of change

Fictional situation

In a voluntary conversation, an adult wishes to talk about a change in everyday life. With the person’s agreement, the professional offers an image as a starting point.

Imagined visual prompt

A path dividing into two directions.

Possible illustrative questions

  1. Observe

    Which detail catches your attention?

  2. Share

    If you wish, what story might this image tell?

  3. Explore

    Is there anything in that story you would like to explore further, or would you prefer to stop here?

Considerations for the professional

Do not infer emotions, conflicts or diagnoses from the choice. Allow the person not to answer, choose another image or stop; if distress arises, assess whether continuing is appropriate within your professional remit.

02 · Group

Group work · One photograph, multiple perspectives

Fictional situation

During a discussion among adults, the facilitator offers the same image to those who wish to participate. Each person may share a brief interpretation, including a purely descriptive one.

Imagined visual prompt

An open window looking out onto a landscape.

Possible illustrative questions

  1. Observe

    What do you notice first?

  2. Share

    What title would you give this photograph, if you wanted to share it?

  3. Explore

    What differences or similarities do we notice among the perspectives we have heard?

Considerations for the professional

Explain the purpose, turn-taking, rules and limits of confidentiality: do not promise absolute confidentiality among participants. Do not require personal stories or ask participants to comment on others’ personalities; respect anyone who chooses not to speak.

03 · Human Resources

Human Resources · Team collaboration

Fictional situation

In an organisational development meeting, a team voluntarily reflects on a shared work process. The image is a metaphor for discussing the process, not workers’ personal characteristics.

Imagined visual prompt

A bridge connecting two riverbanks.

Possible illustrative questions

  1. Observe

    Which part of the image reminds you of a way of collaborating?

  2. Share

    What aspect of our work process would you describe through this metaphor?

  3. Explore

    What small organisational change could we discuss together?

Considerations for the professional

Keep questions focused on work and the stated purpose. Do not collect health or sensitive information, solicit private stories, assign scores or profiles, or use answers for hiring, promotion, exclusion or psychological assessment. Provide an alternative way to participate without penalty.

An example is not a protocol

PdT is proposed as a storytelling resource, not a diagnostic or psychometric test or a stand-alone treatment. These demonstrations do not establish outcomes, efficacy or clinical validation. The site does not request or collect answers. Professionals must assess purpose, competence, context and voluntary participation; intended use and final materials require author approval before release.
