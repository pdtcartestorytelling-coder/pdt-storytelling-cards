01 · Introductory guide

Introductory guide: from images to stories.

Three ideas for understanding PdT without reproducing proprietary practical instructions.

The image as a prompt

A photograph may offer a starting point for a story. It contains no correct answer or mandatory meaning.

Storytelling as a possibility

People may read the same image differently. Listening comes before interpretation.

The setting as a boundary

The professional chooses the approach according to purpose, competencies and suitability. Participation is voluntary.

02 · Professional checklist

Checklist before proposing an activity.

A general preparation reminder, available to read or print from your browser. It is not a PdT activity sheet or suitability assessment.

  • Have I defined the purpose and checked that it falls within my competencies?
  • Have I explained voluntary participation and the option not to answer or to stop?
  • Have I considered accessibility needs, timing and alternatives?
  • Have I explained confidentiality, limits on sharing and any data handling?
  • Have I avoided imposed interpretations, scoring, diagnoses and promises of outcomes?
  • In HR, have I excluded personnel selection and assessment, sensitive data and consequences for declining to participate?

PdT is proposed as a storytelling resource, not a diagnostic or psychometric test or a stand-alone treatment. The intended use and final instructions require approval. The theoretical references do not establish specific efficacy.

03 · Glossary

Essential glossary.

Visual prompt
An image offering an opportunity for observation and association.
Storytelling
A way to tell and give meaning to an experience.
Visual metaphor
Using an image to evoke a meaning beyond its literal description.
Open question
A question allowing different answers without suggesting a correct response.
Setting
The context, purpose, rules and conditions of an activity.
Subjectivity
The personal nature of readings, associations and meanings attributed to an image.
