01 · THE THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

Where the PdT method comes from.

The project grew from a dialogue between the philosophy of cognitive science, psychology and photography. These theoretical contributions inform the project but do not establish its specific efficacy.

01

Philosophy of photography

The distinction between representation and revelation, discussed through Patrick Maynard, and Linda Berman’s work on photography offer ways to reflect on the relationship between images and experience.

02

Storytelling and autobiographical consciousness

The PdT articles draw on Antonio Damasio and Gerald Edelman to reflect on emotions, memory and the construction of self. Storytelling offers a way to give shape to experience.

03

The subjective gaze

Roland Barthes’s concepts of Studium and Punctum help explain why a photograph may hold different meanings for different viewers. They are not categories for classifying people.

02 · THE IMAGES

03 · THE PRACTICAL APPROACH

Observe. Share. Explore.

An illustrative summary of the approach already presented by PdT, not a standardised protocol or reproduction of the original activity sheets.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Choose a photograph and make room for associations without seeking a correct answer.

  2. 02

    Share

    Listen to the person’s words and meanings without imposing interpretations.

  3. 03

    Explore

    Use guided questions flexibly, in line with the professional remit and setting.

The professional defines the purpose, timing and approach, assesses suitability and respects the option not to answer or to stop.

Scope and limitations

PdT is proposed as a storytelling resource. It is not a diagnostic or psychometric test, a stand-alone treatment, or intended to treat or prevent disease. It does not replace professional competencies. The theoretical references do not establish PdT-specific psychometric validity or clinical efficacy. Do not assign scores, profiles or mandatory meanings to images; do not use PdT in HR hiring, promotion or exclusion decisions. Do not solicit sensitive information. The intended use and final instructions require approval before release.
